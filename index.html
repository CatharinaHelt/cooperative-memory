Cooperative environmental memory

A cooperative short-term/long-term memory for autonomous robots that shares environmental knowledge and revises it when evidence changes.

Catharina Helten · RPTU Kaiserslautern-Landau

The problem

Robots perceive the present - but autonomy requires knowledge over time.

Robots perceive the present—but autonomy requires knowledge over time. Instead of starting from zero with every sensor update, robots should build on experience. Cooperative environmental memory turns local observations into persistent, shareable knowledge—helping robot teams see beyond individual sensor limits, withstand temporary uncertainty, and respond when the world changes.

About the author

Catharina Helten

PhD researcher · Adaptive Autonomy and Off-Road Robotics Lab · RPTU Kaiserslautern-Landau

My research explores how ideas from cognitive science can help robots use memory, expectations, and uncertainty to adapt in changing environments. I aim to develop cooperative robots that recognize when their knowledge is unreliable, revise and share it, and act safely—especially in challenging off-road settings. Before starting my PhD at RPTU, I worked as a safety engineer at Fraunhofer IESE, which continues to shape my focus on reliable and understandable autonomy.
